Paper towel holders

Adhesive or screws: how to choose an under-cabinet paper towel holder

The best paper towel holder is the one that keeps the roll off the counter. The second-best question is how you feel about four holes in a cabinet you do not own.

What this guide is

A decision guide for mounting method, plus the three measurements that decide whether a holder fits before you order it.

The short answer

If you rent, or you might rearrange the kitchen next year, choose adhesive. If the cabinet underside is bare wood or a solid finish and you intend to stay, screws are the more permanent answer and a full roll will not test the glue.

The holder on this site's register offers both methods in one box, which is the pragmatic choice if you have not decided: install with adhesive first, and keep the screws in the bag.

When adhesive is the right answer

Adhesive mounting is reversible, fast, and leaves a surface you can clean. That is most of its value. It is not magic, and the honest version of the trade-off is that the bond is only as good as the surface underneath it.

A clean, flat, sealed surface holds well. Textured, dusty, oily or freshly painted surfaces do not — and a kitchen cabinet underside is often all three of the last ones at once. Clean it with a degreaser, let it dry fully, and press the pad for longer than feels necessary.

A useful rule: if you would not stick a hook there, do not stick a loaded paper towel rail there.

When to reach for the drill

Drill when the roll is heavy, when the holder will be pulled at regularly, or when the surface is one adhesive will not grip. A full roll plus the downward tug of one-handed tearing is a small but repeated load, and repeated loads are what defeat adhesive pads.

Two practical notes. Check for pipes and wiring before any drill goes near the underside of a cabinet or a wall — a stud finder is cheap and a leak is not. And pilot the holes: cabinet undersides are usually thin panel, which splits readily.

Three measurements to take before you order

Almost every disappointment with an under-cabinet holder is a measurement that was not taken. None of these require tools beyond a tape measure:

  • Clearance. The distance from the cabinet underside to the counter, and from the underside to the front edge. A twelve-inch rail plus a full roll needs room to turn, and a roll that rubs a backsplash will not spin.
  • Rail length versus roll width. Standard kitchen rolls vary. A 12.2-inch rail is the figure the listing gives for this product; measure the roll you actually buy.
  • Depth and door swing. The holder needs to sit far enough back that the cabinet door closes, and far enough forward that the roll clears the door frame.
  • Fixings already present. Look before you order. Many cabinets already have a child-lock, a light strip or a previous owner's screw holes exactly where you were about to put this.

A word about the “multi-use” claim

Listings often describe a rail like this as also being a towel rail, a foil holder or a utensil holder. That is a reasonable description of the shape, not a tested claim, and it is worth separating the two in your head. A rail that holds a paper roll comfortably may not hold a heavy foil box at the same angle.
