Pot racks

How many tiers do you actually need in a pots and pans organizer?

Eight tiers sounds like more storage. It is actually more height, and height is the thing your cabinet is already short of.

Published 2026-11-205 min readReviewed against official sources

What this guide is

How to size a tiered rack from the pans you own and the cabinet you have, including the weight limit most buyers read past.

The short answer

Count the pans you use weekly, not the pans you own. That number, plus one, is your tier count. Everything above that is a shelf for the pans you were keeping for a reason you cannot now remember.

Then check the height. Tiered racks multiply footprint, not floorspace. If the cabinet is 20 inches tall inside and the rack is 21 inches at its tallest option, the rack is the wrong shape no matter how many tiers it has.

Measure height first, then width

The rack on this site's register is sold in eight heights, from 13.78 to 23.62 inches, and four finishes. That range exists because cabinets do not standardise, and it is the single most useful thing to check before ordering.

Measure the inside of the cabinet at the point the rack will sit — not the doorway, and not the shelf above the one you are planning to use. Then subtract a couple of centimetres so the tallest pan clears the shelf when you lift it out.

The weight sentence most people skip

The listing for this rack describes it as fitting lightweight cookware up to 11 inches. That is a limitation stated by the seller, and it is the most important line on the page for anyone with cast iron, a heavy stockpot or a stack of enamelled pans.

This site records that sentence exactly as written. It does not extend it, soften it or imply that a heavier pan will be fine because the rack looks sturdy. If your pans are heavy, the honest move is to look for a rack the seller rates for that weight.

Practical translation: an eight-tier rack is not a licence to load eight heavy pans. Tiers are positions, not a weight rating.

A five-minute mock-up beats a spec sheet

Before ordering, build the rack out of books. Stack two or three pans on a low pile and see whether the cabinet door still closes, whether the shelf above still takes what it took before, and whether you can reach the top pan without moving the bottom one.

If the mock-up is annoying, the real thing will be too. This is the entire argument for tiers: vertical separation makes the bottom pan reachable. If the mock-up does not produce that feeling, the problem is not the tier count.

Why the variation list is worth reading

Height and finish options on one listing mean the reviews and the photos are not necessarily of the product you are buying. A silver 21-inch rack and a black 14-inch rack can share a page, a rating and a question-and-answer section while being different products in the box.

When you read anything on a listing, confirm which variation it refers to first. That applies to this site's tables too: the specifications here are for the base listing, and a different height is a different item.
